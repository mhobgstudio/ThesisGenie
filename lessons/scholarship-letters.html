The STEP Framework

Use Situation, Task, Edit, Polish to create scholarship letters that stand out:

Step 1: Situation — Research the Scholarship

Before writing anything, gather information:

  • What does the scholarship value? (Academic excellence, leadership, community service?)
  • Who are the previous recipients? (What backgrounds do they have?)
  • What is the selection criteria?
  • What is the word/character limit?

Step 2: Task — Generate Your First Draft

Use this AI prompt template:

You are an expert scholarship application writer. I need a compelling
personal statement for the [SCHOLARSHIP NAME] at [INSTITUTION].

About me:
- Program: [YOUR PROGRAM]
- GPA: [YOUR GPA]
- Key achievements: [LIST 3-5 ACHIEVEMENTS]
- Research interests: [YOUR INTERESTS]
- Career goals: [YOUR GOALS]

The scholarship values: [VALUES FROM RESEARCH]
Word limit: [X] words

Write a personal statement that:
1. Opens with a compelling hook
2. Demonstrates alignment with the scholarship's values
3. Shows specific achievements with impact
4. Connects your past to your future goals
5. Closes with a memorable statement

Tone: Confident but not arrogant. Specific, not generic.

Step 3: Edit — Make It Yours

This is the most important step. The AI draft is a starting point:

  1. Remove generic phrases — "I am passionate about..." → describe a specific moment that shows passion
  2. Add specific examples — Replace vague claims with concrete achievements
  3. Inject your voice — Would your friends recognize this as your writing?
  4. Check facts — Ensure all dates, names, and achievements are accurate
  5. Align with criteria — Does every paragraph connect to what the scholarship values?

Step 4: Polish — The Final Pass

  • Read aloud — does it flow naturally?
  • Check word count — stay within limits
  • Proofread for grammar and spelling
  • Get a second opinion — ask a mentor or peer to review
⚠️ Important: Never submit an unedited AI draft. Scholarship committees read hundreds of applications — they can spot generic AI-generated text instantly. Your unique experiences and voice are what set you apart.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Being too general: "I want to make a difference" → "I want to develop mobile health apps for rural clinics in the Ashanti Region"
  • Repeating your CV: The letter should tell a story, not list achievements
  • Ignoring the prompt: Answer what they ask, not what you want to say
  • Writing for everyone: Write specifically for THIS scholarship
